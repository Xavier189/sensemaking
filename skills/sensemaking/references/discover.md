# Discover — Find a Direction and Basis for Choosing

Use Discover within the exploration loop when the user lacks a stable goal, vocabulary, examples, or the knowledge needed to choose. Do not reopen a clear goal just because its implementation has unexamined effects; use [context-and-impact.md](context-and-impact.md) for those. The aim is not to extract a hidden requirement. It is to make meaningful directions visible and give the user a basis for comparing them.

## Frame

Do not begin with a single claim about “what the user actually wants.” That is appropriate only after evidence exists and otherwise anchors the session to the agent's first guess.

Start from what is real now:

- a frustration, desire, curiosity, obligation, recent event, or opportunity;
- what the user already knows and where their experience stops;
- references they like or dislike, even if they cannot explain why;
- constraints that are real now rather than imagined implementation details.

Reflect this starting point and name the type of uncertainty: missing direction, missing vocabulary, missing examples, or missing judgment criteria.

## Adapt as you go

When the user has rich experience or does not want suggestions, draw out their own thinking. When they are unfamiliar or ask for a recommendation, research and teach first. Otherwise trade hypotheses and examples. Shift when the current approach stops helping, without announcing a mode.

## Discover loop

### 1. Map the known and unknown

Identify, in plain language:

- what the user knows they want;
- what they know they have not decided;
- what they might recognize only after seeing it;
- blind spots they lack the background to name.

Do not expose this as a four-box worksheet unless it helps. Use it to decide the next move.

### 2. Perform a blind-spot pass when needed

If the user repeatedly says “不知道/你定”, stop asking them to choose. Explain the important dimensions, research factual unknowns, and show what good or bad outcomes look like.

Keep the teaching decision-relevant. The goal is not a domain lecture; it is to give the user enough basis to react intelligently.

To surface needs the user would not think to request, ask yourself what a top practitioner in this domain would do here that was not asked for, which raw material they would start from instead of a digested summary, which methods or tools they would use, and what other than the agent or the user would show the result is right. Offer what this reveals as candidates and leave the user's choices open.

### 3. Generate plausible frames

When the opportunity can be read in more than one consequential way, offer interpretations that differ in the change they try to create, who benefits, what they optimize, and what they give up. Difference of wording is not a new frame. Invite the user to combine or reject them. Do not label one “recommended” until evidence or user values justify it.

### 4. Use recognition over recall

When language is the bottleneck, make the choice concrete:

- examples or counterexamples;
- comparable products, documents, workflows, or experiences;
- a rough outline, mockup, scenario, sample output, or cheap prototype;
- two contrasting versions that isolate one decision.

Ask what the user reacts to and why. Their reaction is evidence about criteria they could not state beforehand.

### 5. Diverge and converge deliberately

Do not narrow merely because several ideas now exist. Continue while new frames reveal new values or the user is still learning what is possible.

Begin convergence when the user can compare directions using criteria they understand. Help shortlist, combine, or reject frames; record why. If the user wants to remain exploratory, that is a valid completion state.

## Questions that help

Ask about experience or values rather than implementation parameters: what made this timely, what would be different if it went well, which example feels close, or which cost they would rather accept. Avoid generic prompts such as “What are your requirements?”, long questionnaires, and multiple-choice options the user lacks the knowledge to evaluate.

## Completion and handoff

Discover completes with one of:

- a chosen direction;
- a shortlist plus comparison criteria;
- an explicit exploratory hypothesis to test with research or prototype;
- a deliberate decision not to pursue the space yet.

When a durable summary helps, state the direction, the criteria, and what is still open in ordinary prose. If the chosen direction now contains material decision forks, transition to Clarify. Do not jump directly to implementation planning unless the user's request and downstream workflow require it.
